# APT Principles

APT has one framework with three kinds of guidance. The distinction prevents a public navigation section or a specialist domain from becoming a competing pillar.

## Four Pillars

- [Thinking](thinking/README.md) — frame the problem, audience, evidence, outcomes, assumptions, and tradeoffs.
- [Design](design/README.md) — define coherent behavior, journeys, states, and acceptance criteria.
- [Architecture](architecture/README.md) — assign boundaries, responsibilities, contracts, and ownership.
- [Execution](execution/README.md) — deliver, validate, release, operate, and learn in small evidence-backed increments.

## Lifecycle Practices

Lifecycle practices make the pillars repeatable: [System Standards](system-standards/README.md), [Quality and Testing](execution/quality-and-testing.md), [Release and Change Management](execution/release-and-change-management.md), [Operations and Support](execution/operations-and-support.md), and [Knowledge and Learning](execution/knowledge-and-learning.md).

## Cross-Cutting Overlays

[Security and Risk](security-risk/README.md) and [AI and Agents](ai/README.md) apply across every pillar and lifecycle practice. They are never optional final checks or isolated delivery stages.
