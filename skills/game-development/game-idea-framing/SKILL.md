# Game Idea Framing

## Purpose

Turn an exciting or vague idea into one beginner-manageable game concept.

## When to Use

Use at project start, when several ideas compete, or when the intended experience is unclear.

## Inputs

Player, desired feeling, inspiration, constraints, learning goal, target device, available time, and evidence or assumptions.

## Process

1. Write the player promise and game idea in one sentence each.
2. Name the core action, challenge, feedback, and ending.
3. Separate verified constraints from assumptions.
4. Compare at most three concepts by learning value and prototype cost.
5. Select one concept and define its smallest test.
6. Record owner, cut list, next playable increment, and open questions.

## Outputs

Completed game concept, decision rationale, evidence, assumptions, success signal, cut list, and first prototype question.

## Quality Bar

A beginner can explain who plays, what they repeatedly do, why it matters, and what will not be built yet.
